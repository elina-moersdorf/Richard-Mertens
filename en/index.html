Divorce

From the one-year separation period to filing for divorce: I work out with you what needs to be settled and represent your interests.

Divorce

Describe your matter

I support you throughout the entire divorce process. You can come to me for information on all the legal consequences of a divorce as early as the point at which you are planning to separate.

You can also seek my advice after receiving a divorce application. I am always open with you about the extent to which legal representation is necessary for you – above all with regard to the expected costs. Divorce proceedings do require a lawyer, i.e. the divorce application must be filed through a lawyer. However, you do not need to be represented by a lawyer to consent to the divorce application. In that case, you should at least seek advice on the procedure and, above all, on the consequences of not being legally represented.

During the consultation, I inform you about the equalisation of pension rights and all other ways of enforcing your financial claims or defending against unjustified demands, for example regarding maintenance during separation or the equalisation of accrued gains. I also represent you in international divorces, with a particular focus on Russia and the CIS states as well as the Islamic legal sphere.

Domestic violence

Protection for you and your children: I represent you in protection proceedings and can advise you even before you apply to the court.

Domestic violence

Describe your matter

I represent you in matters under the German Violence Protection Act (Gewaltschutzgesetz). If you have already submitted an application to the court’s legal applications office (Rechtsantragsstelle), you can contact me for an initial consultation. I also act for clients receiving legal aid. Reporting the matter to the police or applying for protection measures usually also means the end of the personal relationship between the partners.

In cases of domestic violence, you can also seek legal advice before applying to the court. As a rule, protection applications should be filed directly with the court’s legal applications office. I also represent you in any criminal proceedings connected with domestic violence.

If you are in immediate danger, please call the emergency number 110 straight away.

Custody and contact

Where the children will live and how contact with both parents will work: together, we look for solutions that are, above all, right for the children.

Custody and contact

Describe your matter

Proceedings concerning parental custody and contact are a particularly sensitive matter for parents. These proceedings are primarily about the interests not of the parents but of the child, who usually suffers most from the situation. I accompany you throughout the proceedings and am at your side as a reliable and knowledgeable source of support.

In the emotionally charged situation of parents in conflict over their child, I work towards a solution in every case, such as a court settlement or a contractual contact agreement. Even where parents are at odds, a joint solution generally serves the child’s welfare and your interests better than a one-sided decision by the family court. Where cooperation reaches its limits, however, I will of course use every procedural means available to protect your interests and the child’s welfare.

Maintenance and assets

Child, separation or spousal maintenance, accrued gains and shared assets: I check what you are entitled to and which claims are justified.

Maintenance and assets

Describe your matter

Family law recognises a number of different types of maintenance – child maintenance, maintenance for relatives, spousal maintenance and so on. I check whether you are entitled to maintenance and how you can enforce it. Equally, I represent you if you are facing unjustified maintenance claims or would like to have the amount of a claim reviewed.

What all types of maintenance have in common is that they are calculated on the basis of income. For this purpose, the person potentially entitled to maintenance has a right to information against the person liable to pay. I support you in providing or obtaining this information. The income determined on that basis defines the maintenance obligation. It is important to look closely at which items may be deducted from income. Many maintenance debtors try to reduce their maintenance burden by understating their income. Which items are actually deductible depends on the statutory requirements and the case law of the courts. I will explain this to you in a consultation.

I also represent you in matters concerning advance maintenance payments (Unterhaltsvorschuss). These are paid by the advance maintenance offices to those in need when minor children live with one of the parents. The parent who does not care for the child often faces high repayment claims from the advance maintenance office – frequently years later. In many cases, however, this debt can be reduced considerably with legal representation. You can book a consultation to find out more about an advance maintenance matter.

I can also support you with regard to assets. In a divorce, it is not the entire property that is divided but only the accrued gains – that is, what a spouse has gained during the marriage. I establish what actually has to be equalised – for example with regard to a house, savings or a shared flat – and work towards a fair division.

Legal aid

Low income? I apply for legal aid on your behalf so that costs do not stand in your way.

Legal aid

Describe your matter

If your income is not sufficient, I will apply for legal aid (Verfahrenskostenhilfe) on your behalf. To do so, you need to complete the form on your personal and financial circumstances. I am happy to help if you have any questions about it. You can find guidance on completing the form (in German) at www.pkh-formular.de.

With legal aid, the state covers the court fees and my fees – depending on your income, either in full or in return for instalments. As a rule, you are entitled to legal aid if you receive social benefits (e.g. citizen’s income or basic income support). You can also receive legal aid if you have a low income. In that case, the court may order you to pay in instalments.

International family law

As a lawyer working in family law, I advise and represent you on cross-border and international family law matters. Consultations can also be held in German or Russian.

International family law

Describe your matter

A foreign connection can arise in various ways, for example through a marriage abroad followed by the spouses moving to Germany, through one or both spouses holding a foreign nationality, or simply through a planned move abroad. The consequences vary and depend on several factors. A foreign connection is particularly complex in the case of countries outside the European Union, such as Russia, Iran, Vietnam or others.

Legal advice is usually advisable here, because the effects can differ greatly from country to country. The possible consequences are often not easy for spouses to recognise. In the event of separation or divorce, this can lead to unpleasant surprises. The possible consequences can be cushioned in advance by agreements, for example a marital agreement (Ehevertrag) with a clause choosing German law. I can help you with this.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to your questions

What does legal representation cost?

I charge in accordance with the German Lawyers’ Remuneration Act (RVG). The fees depend on the value of the matter, which in turn depends on the type of proceedings. There are numerous online calculators for divorce costs, for example the divorce cost calculator of the German Lawyers’ Association (Deutscher Anwaltverein). I can give you an estimate of the likely costs during the initial consultation.

Will my legal expenses insurance cover the costs?

In family law matters, legal expenses insurance usually offers rather limited cover. However, there are premium policies, for example with family law cover. I can check for you whether your insurer will pay. I advise against taking out a policy specifically for an upcoming divorce – even if it is still a year away.

Will I lose half of my property in a divorce?

That is a widespread misconception. In a divorce, only the gains accrued during the marriage are equalised. In other words, only what a spouse has gained during the marriage is shared. If, for example, someone brings a property into the marriage and at the end still owns only that property, at most its increase in value can be shared – but not the property itself.

Doesn’t bringing in a lawyer just escalate things?

I hear this a lot. If your partner warns that bringing in a lawyer means escalation, the personal relationship has usually already broken down to the point where you need professional support.

For my part, I try to de-escalate the conflict – above all by favouring out-of-court settlements. Sometimes I also advise turning to a counselling service first. In a separation, an initial consultation is helpful in almost every case.

What must always be kept in mind when divorcing a foreign marriage?

In Germany, the divorce of a foreign marriage generally follows the same rules as a domestic divorce. This means that a one-year separation period must be observed. Far more important is the equalisation of pension rights (Versorgungsausgleich) in the divorce, which divides the pension entitlements of both spouses between them. It is common for spouses to have moved to Germany because one of them found work here. In that case, the spouse who did not work, or worked only a little, would benefit from the equalisation of pension rights.

But be careful: in foreign marriages, the equalisation of pension rights is often carried out only on application. This means that the court will then not carry it out automatically, but only if you apply for it separately. If no application is made, no equalisation of pension rights takes place. You should therefore have it checked in advance whether you need to make an application.

Islamic dower (mahr, sadaq, morning gift)Can I sue in a German court for a mahr agreed in Iran?

Agreeing a dower is common practice in many countries shaped by Islam. In Germany, dowers are no longer customary. However, dowers agreed abroad are generally valid in Germany. This means that you can sue for a dower before a German court, or be sued for payment of a dower in Germany – provided that the German courts also have international jurisdiction.

Whether German courts have jurisdiction must be clarified in each individual case. Typically, jurisdiction exists because the spouses are resident in Germany. The German courts have already issued numerous decisions on dowers, for example on Iranian, Tunisian or Turkish dowers.

Islamic dower (mahr, sadaq, morning gift)On what grounds can payment be refused?

In individual cases, the promise of a dower may be contrary to public morals (section 138 of the German Civil Code) or to German public policy (ordre public). Specifically, the husband will argue that the promise places such a financial burden on him that payment cannot reasonably be expected of him. As a rule, however, this attempt will not succeed.

Even if a foreign dower can be claimed before a German court, the foreign substantive law is usually applied. This means that German law does not apply at all to the specific questions concerning the dower itself. Many foreign legal systems do not provide for any exclusion of dowers on grounds of over-indebtedness. The promise of a high dower can therefore be valid in individual cases.

Under Iranian law, dowers of no more than 110 full Bahar Azadi gold coins are in principle always valid (Art. 22, sentence 1 of the Iranian Family Protection Act). Promises exceeding this are also valid; however, when it comes to enforcement, the husband can plead his lack of financial capacity for the amounts in excess.
